Five days on from part 1. The shell is ready, but before the racks can go in we need a carrier circuit, cable pathways, and somewhere for the building services to politely move out of the way.

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/1.jpg" alt="Fibre splice enclosure mounted to a wall with grey cable tray, conduit, and pink slack coiled around it">
  <figcaption>The plant room splice point where OpenReach hand off our fibre</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/3.jpg" alt="Open plant cupboards with red fire valve and hot water pipework, plasterer's bucket on the floor">
  <figcaption>The plant cupboards where the fibre comes in from the building riser</figcaption>
</figure>

A BT OpenReach engineer is blowing fibre from the plant room splice point up to the comms room. The plant cupboard above sits between the building riser and our space, and the splice enclosure is where the carrier's responsibility ends and ours begins.

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/2.jpg" alt="Under-floor void inside the comms room showing wire cable basket and steel busbar trunking running between rack feet">
  <figcaption>Inside the comms room: cable basket and trunking under the raised floor</figcaption>
</figure>

Inside the comms room, there isn't much space under that raised floor. The original cable basket and ducting had to be removed and re-routed to make room for everything that actually needed to live down there.

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/7.jpg" alt="Close-up of floor void next to a rack foot showing cable tray and a thick insulated copper pipe running under the tiles">
  <figcaption>Insulated heating pipework running directly under the rack footprint</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/5.jpg" alt="Partially assembled rack frame on the raised floor with tiles lifted, copper pipework visible in the void, engineer kneeling alongside">
  <figcaption>First rack frame going up over a floor void that was never designed to carry a comms room</figcaption>
</figure>

Those pipes are a problem, and the raised floor offers only 11cm of clearance. Every cable tray, busbar, and earth strap that has to live under the floor is competing with pre-existing building services for a sliver of vertical room.

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/6.jpg" alt="Long black anti-static matting laid across freshly tiled office floor, leading from the open-plan space toward the comms room doorway">
  <figcaption>Matting laid along the route the patch cables will take from the floor boxes back to the comms room</figcaption>
</figure>

The matting traces the path the patch cables will take to reach the comms room. Setting it out now lets the cabling contractor pull the bulk runs without scuffing the freshly-laid floor tiles in the office area.

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/4.jpg" alt="Long open-plan office space with ceiling rafts installed, studwork partitions to one side, and windows along the right wall">
  <figcaption>A quick look at the wider office area as it comes together in parallel</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure>
  <img src="/uploads/fitout/2014-07-28/8.jpg" alt="Three blue and grey rack frames standing in a row in the comms room, labelled in marker as Cab 1, Cab 2, and Cab 3">
  <figcaption>Three cabinets positioned, labelled, and ready for the kit</figcaption>
</figure>

Cab 1 will be the patching and comms cabinet, Cab 2 and Cab 3 will hold the development and infrastructure servers we've kept on-prem.
